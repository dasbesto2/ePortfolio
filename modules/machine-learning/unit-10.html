Unit 10 — Natural Language Processing

LO1 Ethics & professionalismLO2 Dataset applicability

Transformer-based NLP (BERT, GPT, T5) has reshaped text generation; this unit's e-portfolio activity is the summary of Collaborative Discussion 2, opened in Unit 8 from Hutson's (2021) Nature piece on AI "robo-writers".

Discussion 2: legal & ethical views on AI writing — summary post

The discussion begins with an initial post arguing that large language models (LLMs) are reshaping writing across administrative, academic and professional contexts by shifting the writer's role from direct composition to the supervision of machine-generated text (Hutson, 2025). While AI can improve efficiency in routine tasks, it also introduces risks because LLMs generate plausible language without genuine understanding. Building on this, Patrick argued that overreliance may already be occurring rather than being a future concern, citing evidence that users frequently accept AI-generated output with minimal editing (Noy and Zhang, 2023) and linking this to automation bias, where fluent output suppresses the scrutiny it most requires. Nathalie further challenged the assumption that administrative writing is necessarily low-stakes, noting that errors in AI-generated customer-service responses or performance reviews can have real consequences (Sookman, 2024). Nathalie and I both acknowledged AI's value for non-native English speakers while stressing the need for factual verification and human oversight. On creative writing, I argued AI is most useful as a brainstorming partner, while Nathalie highlighted evidence that AI assistance can improve individual outputs while reducing diversity across creative work as a whole (Doshi and Hauser, 2024).

Overall, the discussion converges on the view that AI can enhance productivity, but only when human judgement remains central. It also raised the question of whether disclosure alone is enough to ensure quality: recent evidence suggests disclosure reveals AI involvement without establishing the reliability of the result (Unal, 2026), and that trust depends instead on how AI-generated content is verified and integrated into human work (Bădescu et al., 2026).
