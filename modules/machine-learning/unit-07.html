Unit 7 — Introduction to Artificial Neural Networks

Dataset separability and the challenges it creates for learning algorithms — a report spanning three notebooks: a hand-set perceptron, a trainable perceptron learning AND, and a multi-layer perceptron (sigmoid) learning XOR.

Ex2 — Perceptron, AND4 binary pairsConverged in 4 passes to weights [0.5, 0.5]; all 4 cases correctLinearly separable
Ex3 — MLP, XOR (sigmoid)Same 4 pairs, XOR targetMean error fell ~0.50 → ~0.009 over 400,000 epochs after fixing a variable-name bug; correctly predicted 0,1,1,0Not linearly separable

What running the notebooks (not just reading them) showed

  • Separability, not size, drives difficulty. AND is linearly separable, so a single perceptron solves it in four passes; XOR is not, which is exactly why Ex3 needs a hidden layer to bend the input space before a line can separate the classes.
  • A bug can hide inside a "working" notebook. Ex3's training loop referenced an undefined variable (weights1 vs. weights_1), which silently stopped learning after epoch 1 — the network looked trained but was still at random-initialisation performance. Fixing the typo and re-running produced the real result above.
  • The MLP is far more parameter-sensitive than the deterministic perceptron: initialisation, learning rate (0.6) and epoch count (400,000) all had to be right, and sigmoid's small derivative (max 0.25) is a preview of the vanishing-gradient problem in deeper networks.
  • Toy datasets can hide data-preparation issues that reappear at scale — Ex1's raw unscaled inputs only worked because the weights were hand-picked to compensate.

Full report (purpose, method, results table, and the applicability/challenges discussion above) is the primary artefact for this unit; condensed here to the findings that carry forward into later units.
