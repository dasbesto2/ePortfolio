A conceptual unit: comparing two interactive visualisations of k-means, then a worked Jaccard-distance calculation by hand.

Wiki activity — comparing two k-means animations

Both sources illustrate k-means but emphasise different parts of the same logic. The Naftali Harris visualisation presents the algorithm as an alternating two-step loop — reassign every point to its nearest centroid, then move each centroid to the mean of its assigned points, repeating until nothing moves — and its Voronoi-shaded regions make the "nearest centroid" rule visible. The Shabalin visualisation, by contrast, shows a converged outcome: three tight groups with lines drawn from each point to its centroid, highlighting the mean-based pull of the algorithm, with a stray point from a poor initial choice showing how initialisation can change the final grouping.

In short, one visualisation explains the mechanism (partitioning space by proximity, iterating, and the pitfalls of initialisation and choosing k), the other shows the payoff (well-separated clusters settling cleanly). Both rest on the same insight: points in a cluster should sit close to its centre.

e-Portfolio activity — Jaccard distance

How it works

  • Gender is a symmetric attribute, so it is excluded from the Jaccard calculation entirely.
  • Fever, Cough and Test-1 to Test-4 are treated as asymmetric binary attributes: Y/P → 1 (positive/present), N → 0 (negative/absent), A → missing (that attribute is skipped for the pair being compared, since it wasn't tested).
  • For asymmetric binary variables, (0,0) matches don't count toward similarity (a shared absence isn't meaningful), so:

d(i,j) = (r + s) / (q + r + s)

where q = attributes where both are 1, r = i is 1 and j is 0, s = i is 0 and j is 1.

Worked example (Jack, Mary): Test-4 is skipped for Jack (A) and Test-2 is skipped for Mary (A), leaving Fever, Cough, Test-1 and Test-3. Fever and Test-1 are both positive (q = 2), Test-3 differs (r + s = 1) and Cough is a shared absence that is ignored, giving 1 / 3 ≈ 0.333.

Result

PairJaccard distance
Jack, Mary0.333
Jack, Jim0.667
Jim, Mary0.750

Jack and Mary are the most similar pair; Jim and Mary are the least similar.
